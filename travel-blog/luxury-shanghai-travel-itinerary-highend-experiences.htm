Luxury Shanghai Travel Itinerary: High-End Experiences

Home / Travel Blog / Blog Details

Shanghai is a city that thrives on contrasts. It is a place where ancient traditions meet futuristic skylines, where narrow alleyways known as longtang sit in the shadow of glass-and-steel skyscrapers, and where the humblest street food stall can exist just a few blocks away from a three-Michelin-starred restaurant. For the discerning traveler seeking the pinnacle of luxury, Shanghai offers an intoxicating blend of old-world elegance, cutting-edge modernity, and a level of service that borders on the obsessive. This itinerary is designed not just to show you Shanghai, but to immerse you in its most exclusive corners, its most refined culinary experiences, and its most extravagant accommodations. Forget the budget hostels and the crowded tourist traps. This is Shanghai, reimagined for those who demand the best.

Day 1: The Bund, Art Deco Grandeur, and a River of Lights

Your journey into luxury begins in the heart of Shanghai’s most iconic district: The Bund. But you are not here to jostle with the crowds for a photograph. You are here to feel the history and the power.

Morning: Check-In at The Peninsula Shanghai

Your home for the next few days is not a hotel; it is a landmark. The Peninsula Shanghai, located on the northern end of the Bund, is a masterpiece of Art Deco architecture, a style that defines so much of old Shanghai. The lobby alone, with its soaring ceilings, marble floors, and live classical music, sets a tone of unmatched sophistication. Check into a Grand Premier Suite, where floor-to-ceiling windows offer panoramic views of the Pudong skyline across the Huangpu River. The service is anticipatory; your personal butler will have already noted your preferences for pillow type, morning coffee, and evening turn-down treats. Take a moment to breathe in the view. You have arrived.

Late Morning: A Private Art Deco Walking Tour

Before the city gets too hot or too crowded, step outside with a private guide who specializes in the architectural history of the Bund. This is not a generic tour. Your guide will unlock the doors of buildings that are closed to the public. You will step inside the former headquarters of the Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation (now the Shanghai Pudong Development Bank), marveling at its massive, mosaic-covered dome. You will visit the Peace Hotel, another Art Deco icon, and hear tales of the legendary jazz band that played there through the decades. This is a curated, intellectual experience, a deep dive into the soul of a city that was once the Paris of the East.

Lunch: A Culinary Time Capsule at Yongfoo Elite

For lunch, you will leave the Bund and travel to the former French Concession, a leafy, tranquil district of plane trees and elegant villas. Your destination is Yongfoo Elite, a restaurant and private club housed in a stunning 1912 villa. The setting is an experience in itself—a lush garden, antique furniture, and an atmosphere of hushed elegance. The cuisine is a refined take on Shanghainese classics. Do not miss the Drunken Chicken, the Xiaolongbao (soup dumplings) with a perfectly thin skin, and the sweet and savory Braised Pork Belly. This is not a meal; it is a performance of taste and tradition.

Afternoon: The French Concession by Rickshaw and a Bespoke Suit

After lunch, the best way to explore the French Concession is in a vintage rickshaw, but not the touristy kind. Your hotel will arrange for a private, chauffeur-driven rickshaw that is comfortable and stylish. You will glide down Wukang Road, past the famous Wukang Mansion, and through the quiet, shaded streets that are lined with boutiques and cafes. Your destination is a tailor shop, but not just any tailor. You are heading to a master tailor on Maoming Road, one of the last artisans who can create a bespoke suit or a traditional qipao in 24 hours. The experience is intimate: you choose your fabric from a wall of hundreds of bolts of cashmere, silk, and linen. The tailor takes over 30 measurements. By the time you leave Shanghai, you will have a garment that fits you perfectly, a wearable souvenir of your journey.

Evening: The Bund at Night from a Private Yacht

The Bund is beautiful at any time, but it is truly magical at night when the Pudong skyline lights up like a circuit board. Skip the crowded river cruises. Instead, your hotel concierge has arranged for a private, 50-foot motor yacht. You will board at a private dock near the Waibaidu Bridge. As you glide onto the river, a glass of Krug Champagne in your hand, you will watch the lights of the Oriental Pearl Tower, the Shanghai Tower, and the Jin Mao Building reflect on the water. The city is your stage, and you are the only audience. This is the most romantic, most exclusive way to see Shanghai’s most famous view.

Dinner: A Legendary Feast at Ultraviolet by Paul Pairet

If you are a serious gourmand, there is only one dinner in Shanghai: Ultraviolet by Paul Pairet. This is not a restaurant; it is a multi-sensory theater. You will be picked up from a secret meeting point in a private van. The location of the restaurant itself is a secret. Once inside, you are seated at a single, large table with up to 10 other guests. The walls are projection screens. The music, the lighting, the scents, even the temperature of the room change with each course. The 20-course tasting menu is a journey through Pairet’s imagination, featuring dishes like “Black Truffle” and “The Sound of the Sea.” This is the most expensive and most exclusive dining experience in Shanghai, and it is worth every penny. Book months in advance.

Day 2: Pudong’s Giants, Tea Ceremonies, and a Night at the Opera

Today, you cross the river to explore the futuristic side of Shanghai. It is a day of verticality, of dizzying heights, and of ancient traditions that have been preserved in the midst of modernity.

Morning: A Private Visit to the Shanghai Tower

You have seen the Shanghai Tower from the Bund. Now, you will conquer it. But you are not buying a standard ticket. Your concierge has arranged a private, behind-the-scenes tour. You will take the express elevator to the 118th floor, but your guide will take you to a restricted area on the 125th floor, where the massive, 1,000-ton tuned mass damper is located. You will learn how this engineering marvel keeps the world’s second-tallest building stable in a typhoon. Then, you will be taken to a private observation lounge, away from the crowds, where you can enjoy a cup of tea while looking down at the city from a height of over 2,000 feet. The view is humbling and exhilarating.

Late Morning: A Private Tea Ceremony in the Old City

From the heights of Pudong, you descend into the labyrinthine streets of the Old City, near the Yu Garden. But you are not visiting the garden itself, which is always crowded. Instead, you are heading to a hidden tea house, a private residence that has been in the same family for five generations. Here, a tea master will perform a traditional Gongfu tea ceremony. You will learn about the different varieties of Chinese tea—the floral notes of a Tieguanyin, the earthy depth of a Pu’er, the delicate sweetness of a Longjing. The ceremony is slow, meditative, and deeply peaceful. It is a perfect antidote to the frenetic energy of the city outside.

Lunch: Modern Chinese at Fu He Hui

For lunch, you will return to the French Concession for a meal at Fu He Hui, a temple of vegetarian cuisine. This is not your average vegetarian restaurant. The chef, Tony Lu, creates a tasting menu that is a celebration of seasonal, plant-based ingredients. Each dish is a work of art, presented on handmade ceramics. The flavors are subtle, complex, and deeply satisfying. The setting is minimalist and serene, with a small, private dining room that feels like a Zen garden. This is a meal that will change the way you think about vegetables.

Afternoon: Shopping at Plaza 66 and a Private Art Gallery

After lunch, it is time for some of the most exclusive shopping in Asia. Plaza 66 on Nanjing West Road is the epicenter of luxury retail in Shanghai. Here, you will find flagship stores for Louis Vuitton, Hermès, Gucci, and Chanel, as well as lesser-known luxury brands from around the world. Your personal shopper from the hotel will accompany you, providing access to VIP rooms and private viewing of collections that are not yet available to the public. After you have shopped, you will visit a private art gallery in the M50 art district. This is not a public gallery. It is a collector’s private space, where you can view works by emerging Chinese contemporary artists and, if you are interested, make a purchase. Shanghai’s art scene is one of the most dynamic in the world, and this is your chance to take a piece of it home.

Evening: The Shanghai Grand Theatre and a Post-Show Supper

Tonight, you will experience the cultural side of Shanghai. The Shanghai Grand Theatre, located in People’s Square, is a stunning architectural landmark. Your concierge has secured a box for a performance of a traditional Chinese opera or a world-class ballet. The acoustics are perfect, the seats are plush, and the atmosphere is electric. After the performance, you will head to a very special location for a late supper: the bar at the Waldorf Astoria on the Bund. The bar is called the Long Bar, and it is a replica of the original Long Bar at the Waldorf Astoria in New York. The cocktails are legendary, and the views of the Bund at night are breathtaking. Order a classic Shanghai Sling and soak in the glamour.

Day 3: Water Towns, Silk, and a Farewell Feast

On your final day, you will escape the city center to experience a different side of Shanghai—the ancient water towns that dot the countryside.

Morning: A Private Helicopter to Zhujiajiao

The water towns near Shanghai are beautiful, but they can be crowded. The best way to visit is by private helicopter. Your pilot will pick you up from a helipad in Pudong, and within 20 minutes, you will be landing in Zhujiajiao, a 1,700-year-old water town known as the “Venice of Shanghai.” Your guide will meet you on the landing pad, and you will begin a private walking tour of the town’s canals, stone bridges, and ancient buildings. You will visit a private home that has been in the same family for centuries, and you will take a private gondola ride through the canals, away from the tourist boats.

Lunch: A Family-Style Feast in a Private Garden

For lunch, you will not eat at a restaurant. Instead, you will be invited into the home of a local family who has prepared a traditional, multi-course feast. The food is simple but incredibly fresh: river fish, stir-fried greens, a rich pork belly stew, and freshly made tofu. The family will welcome you as an honored guest, and you will eat in their private garden, surrounded by flowers and the sound of running water. This is a deeply authentic experience, a taste of the real China that few tourists ever see.

Afternoon: The Silk Market and a Private Workshop

On your way back to Shanghai, you will stop at the famous Silk Market. But again, you are skipping the crowded stalls. Your guide will take you to a private workshop on the upper floor, where you can see the entire process of silk production, from the silkworm cocoon to the finished fabric. You will have the opportunity to purchase the highest-quality silk products—shawls, scarves, bedding, and clothing—at prices that are far below what you would find in the West. The quality is unmatched.

Evening: A Farewell Dinner at Mr & Mrs Bund

For your final dinner in Shanghai, you will return to the Bund, but this time to a restaurant that is modern, playful, and utterly sophisticated: Mr & Mrs Bund by Paul Pairet. This is the more casual sibling of Ultraviolet, but it is no less impressive. The menu is a global affair, with dishes like whole roasted chicken, a legendary lemon tart, and the best steak frites in the city. The atmosphere is lively, the wine list is extensive, and the views of the Pudong skyline are stunning. It is the perfect place to raise a glass to your unforgettable journey.

Late Night: A Final Drink at the Flair Rooftop Bar

Your journey ends where it began: with a view. You will head to the Flair Rooftop Bar at the Ritz-Carlton Shanghai, Pudong. This is the highest rooftop bar in Shanghai, offering a 360-degree view of the city. You will order a final cocktail—a classic Negroni or a creative Shanghai-inspired concoction—and you will watch the city lights twinkle below you. The music is soft, the crowd is chic, and the moment is perfect. You have experienced Shanghai in its most luxurious form. You have eaten at its finest tables, slept in its most elegant rooms, and seen its most hidden corners. You leave not just with memories, but with a new understanding of what luxury truly means: it is not about the price tag, but about the quality of the experience, the depth of the connection, and the beauty of the moment. Shanghai has given you all of that. And you will carry it with you, long after you have left its glittering shores.
