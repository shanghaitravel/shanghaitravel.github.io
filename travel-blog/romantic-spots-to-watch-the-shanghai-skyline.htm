Romantic Spots to Watch the Shanghai Skyline

Home / Travel Blog / Blog Details

There is something undeniably magical about Shanghai after dark. When the sun dips below the Huangpu River and the city’s iconic skyline begins to shimmer with a million lights, the energy shifts. The air feels electric, the crowds soften, and the skyline becomes a living canvas of glass, steel, and neon. For couples seeking romance, this city offers an endless array of spots where you can hold hands, sip champagne, and watch the skyline transform into a glittering dreamscape. Whether you are visiting for the first time or have lived here for years, these romantic vantage points will make you fall in love with Shanghai all over again.

The Bund: Where Classic Romance Meets Modern Majesty

The Bund is the most famous waterfront in China, and for good reason. Stretching along the western bank of the Huangpu River, this historic promenade offers an unobstructed view of Pudong’s futuristic skyline. But the Bund is not just a tourist trap—it is a place where love stories unfold every night.

The Best Time to Visit the Bund for Romance

Arrive just before sunset, around 5:30 PM in winter or 6:30 PM in summer. The golden hour casts a warm glow over the colonial-era buildings behind you, while the skyscrapers across the river begin to light up one by one. As the sky deepens into shades of purple and navy, the city transforms. This is the perfect moment to pull your partner close and watch the transition from day to night.

Where to Stand on the Bund

Avoid the crowded main section near the Peace Hotel. Instead, walk south toward the Shiliupu area, where the crowds thin out and the view becomes even more dramatic. There is a small pier near the 16th-century Shiliupu Wharf that offers a quieter spot. Alternatively, head north toward the Huangpu Park area. The trees here frame the skyline beautifully, and the benches along the walkway provide a cozy place to sit and cuddle.

A Secret Tip for the Ultimate Romantic Experience

Book a table at one of the rooftop bars along the Bund. Bars like Flair Rooftop at the Ritz-Carlton or Bar Rouge on the 7th floor of the Bund 18 building offer sweeping views without the crowds. Order a bottle of wine and watch the lights of the Oriental Pearl Tower and the Shanghai Tower twinkle in the distance. If you want something more intimate, try Sir Elly’s Terrace at the Peninsula Hotel. It is quieter, more elegant, and the service is impeccable. The view of Pudong from here is nothing short of breathtaking.

The Huangpu River Cruise: A Floating Love Story

If you want to feel like you are in a movie, take a nighttime cruise on the Huangpu River. The boats glide past both the historic Bund and the futuristic Pudong skyline, offering a 360-degree view of the city’s most iconic landmarks.

Choosing the Right Cruise

There are many cruise options, but for romance, skip the crowded tourist boats and opt for a smaller, more luxurious vessel. Companies like Huangpu River Cruise Co. offer private cabins with glass walls, so you can enjoy the view without the wind. Some cruises even include dinner or champagne. The VIP Night Cruise is worth the extra cost—it lasts about an hour and takes you past the Bund, the Oriental Pearl Tower, and the Shanghai Tower, with live commentary that adds context to the beauty.

What Makes the Cruise So Romantic

The gentle rocking of the boat, the cool breeze off the river, and the ever-changing panorama of lights create an atmosphere that is both intimate and exhilarating. As you sail under the Waibaidu Bridge, the city lights reflect on the water like scattered diamonds. Hold your partner’s hand and whisper something sweet—the sound of the water and the distant hum of the city will make it feel like the world has faded away.

When to Go

The best time for a cruise is between 8:00 PM and 9:30 PM. The skyline is fully lit, but the river traffic is lighter than during peak hours. If you want to avoid the crowds entirely, book a late-night cruise at 10:00 PM. The city is quieter, and the lights seem even more brilliant against the dark sky.

The Shanghai Tower Observation Deck: Love Above the Clouds

For couples who crave height and drama, the Shanghai Tower Observation Deck is an unforgettable experience. At 632 meters, it is the tallest building in China and the second tallest in the world. The observation deck on the 118th floor offers a panoramic view of the entire city, and on a clear night, you can see for miles.

Why It Is Romantic

Standing at the top of the world with your loved one is a powerful feeling. The observation deck has floor-to-ceiling windows that curve gently, giving you the sensation of floating above the city. The lights below look like a carpet of stars, and the feeling of being so high up, yet so safe, is deeply romantic.

The Best Time to Visit

Go just before sunset. The ticket price is the same, but you get two shows for the price of one: the daytime view of the city and the gradual transition into night. The observation deck gets crowded during peak hours, so try to arrive around 4:30 PM. You will have plenty of time to find a good spot, take photos, and watch the sunset before the lights come on.

A Special Touch

The Shanghai Tower also has a skywalk on the 119th floor, which is enclosed but offers a slightly different angle. For the ultimate romantic gesture, book a private viewing session. Some packages include champagne and a personal guide, making the experience feel exclusive and intimate.

The French Concession: A Hidden Gem with a Vintage Vibe

While the Bund and Pudong offer modern grandeur, the French Concession offers a quieter, more intimate kind of romance. This leafy neighborhood is filled with tree-lined streets, Art Deco buildings, and hidden bars that feel like secret gardens.

The Best Rooftop Bars in the French Concession

For a view of the skyline that is less obvious but equally stunning, head to The Roof at the Wukang Hotel. This rooftop bar overlooks the old French Concession, with the Pudong skyline visible in the distance. The bar is cozy, with fairy lights and comfortable seating, making it perfect for a date. Another great option is Speak Low, a hidden speakeasy that has a rooftop terrace with a view of the city. The cocktails here are world-class, and the atmosphere is mysterious and romantic.

Why the French Concession is Special

The French Concession offers a different perspective on the Shanghai skyline. Here, you are not staring directly at the skyscrapers—you are seeing them from a distance, framed by old trees and historic buildings. This creates a sense of nostalgia and intimacy that is hard to find elsewhere. After your drink, take a walk along Wukang Road or Fuxing Road. The streets are quiet at night, and the old buildings glow with soft light. It is the perfect place for a late-night stroll and a kiss under the lanterns.

The Lujiazui Skyline: A View from the Other Side

Most tourists view the skyline from the Bund, but the view from the Pudong side is equally stunning. Standing in Lujiazui, you look back at the historic Bund, which appears like a golden necklace along the river.

The Best Spot in Lujiazui

The Lujiazui Green is a small park near the base of the Shanghai Tower. It is not well-known among tourists, so it is often quiet. From here, you can see the entire Bund skyline reflected in the river. The park has benches and walking paths, making it a great place for a picnic or a quiet evening. Bring a blanket, some snacks, and a bottle of wine, and watch the lights of the Bund dance on the water.

The Pearl Tower Observation Deck

For a more classic experience, visit the Oriental Pearl Tower’s observation deck. It is older than the Shanghai Tower, but it offers a unique perspective. The deck is lower, so you feel more connected to the city. The view of the Bund from here is iconic, and the rotating restaurant at the top is a romantic spot for dinner. The food is not the best in Shanghai, but the view more than makes up for it.

Hidden Rooftops and Secret Spots

Shanghai is full of hidden rooftops that offer stunning views without the crowds. These are the spots that locals keep to themselves, and they are perfect for couples who want something unique.

The Captain’s Hostel Rooftop

This is a bit of a legend among budget travelers, but it is also a romantic spot. The rooftop bar at the Captain’s Hostel on the Bund offers a cheap drink and a fantastic view. It is not fancy, but it is authentic. The crowd is young and lively, and the view of Pudong is just as good as from the expensive bars nearby.

The M on the Bund Rooftop

M on the Bund is a well-known restaurant, but its rooftop is often overlooked. The terrace offers a side view of the Bund and the river, with a more intimate setting than the main promenade. The cocktails are excellent, and the service is attentive. It is a great place for a romantic dinner before heading down to the Bund for a walk.

The Bar at the Waldorf Astoria

The Waldorf Astoria on the Bund is a historic hotel with a stunning bar. The Long Bar on the ground floor is famous, but the rooftop bar is the real gem. It offers a view of the Bund and the river, with a classic, old-world atmosphere. The bartenders make excellent martinis, and the setting is perfect for a sophisticated date.

The Best Time of Year for a Romantic Skyline View

Shanghai’s skyline is beautiful year-round, but certain seasons offer unique experiences.

Spring (March to May)

Spring is mild and pleasant, with clear skies and comfortable temperatures. The cherry blossoms along the Bund add a touch of pink to the view, making it even more romantic. This is the best time for outdoor spots like the Bund and the Lujiazui Green.

Summer (June to August)

Summer is hot and humid, but the nights are long and the skyline is vibrant. The rooftop bars come alive, and the river cruises are popular. The only downside is the occasional rain, but even a light drizzle can make the city lights shimmer beautifully. If you visit in summer, bring a light jacket for the evening breeze.

Autumn (September to November)

Autumn is the best season for photography. The skies are clear, the air is crisp, and the colors of the sunset are spectacular. The Bund is less crowded than in summer, and the rooftop bars are still open. This is the perfect time for a romantic evening.

Winter (December to February)

Winter is cold, but the skyline is magical. The lights seem brighter against the dark, cold sky, and the crowds are thin. The rooftop bars are mostly indoor, but the views are still stunning. If you visit in winter, bundle up and enjoy the quiet beauty of the city.

Practical Tips for a Romantic Evening

  • Dress for the weather. Even in summer, the river breeze can be cool. Bring a light jacket or a scarf.
  • Book in advance. Popular spots like the Shanghai Tower and the river cruises sell out quickly, especially on weekends.
  • Avoid peak hours. The Bund is busiest between 7:00 PM and 9:00 PM. If you want a quieter experience, go later or earlier.
  • Bring a camera. The skyline is incredibly photogenic, and you will want to capture the memories.
  • Don’t forget to look up. Sometimes the most romantic moments are the unexpected ones—a shooting star, a passing cloud, or the reflection of the city in your partner’s eyes.

Final Thoughts

Shanghai’s skyline is more than just a collection of buildings. It is a symbol of the city’s energy, ambition, and beauty. For couples, it is a backdrop for love stories—a place where you can hold hands, share secrets, and watch the world glow. Whether you choose the historic Bund, the dizzying heights of the Shanghai Tower, or the hidden rooftops of the French Concession, the skyline will always be there, waiting to welcome you with open arms and a million lights.

So go ahead. Find your spot. Order a drink. Take a deep breath. And let the city of lights work its magic.
