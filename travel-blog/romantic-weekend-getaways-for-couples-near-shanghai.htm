Romantic Weekend Getaways for Couples Near Shanghai

Home / Travel Blog / Blog Details

Shanghai may be a dazzling metropolis of neon lights, towering skyscrapers, and endless energy, but even the most devoted urbanites need a break. For couples, escaping the city’s relentless rhythm for a weekend is not just a luxury—it is a necessity. Fortunately, within a two-to-three-hour radius of Shanghai, there lies a treasure trove of romantic destinations. Whether you crave ancient water towns, serene lakes, lush mountains, or coastal breezes, these getaways offer the perfect backdrop for reconnection. Let’s explore the best romantic weekend escapes that promise to reignite the spark.

Suzhou: The Venice of the East with a Touch of Elegance

Just a 30-minute high-speed train ride from Shanghai, Suzhou is the quintessential romantic retreat. Known for its classical gardens, silk heritage, and tranquil canals, this city whispers romance at every corner.

Stroll Through the Classical Gardens

The Humble Administrator’s Garden and the Lingering Garden are UNESCO World Heritage sites that feel like living paintings. For couples, these gardens offer secluded pavilions, winding corridors, and lotus-filled ponds. Visit during spring when the cherry blossoms bloom, or in autumn when the maple leaves turn crimson. The key is to go early in the morning or late in the afternoon to avoid crowds. Hold hands as you cross the zigzag bridges—each turn reveals a new perspective, much like a long-term relationship.

A Night on Pingjiang Road

Pingjiang Road is Suzhou’s most romantic ancient street. Cobblestone paths, traditional white-washed buildings, and canals lined with willow trees create a dreamy atmosphere. At dusk, the lanterns light up, casting a warm glow on the water. Couples can take a private gondola ride, sipping tea while listening to the gentle splash of oars. Stop by a tiny teahouse for a pot of Biluochun green tea and order a plate of sweet osmanthus cakes. The slow pace here is a perfect antidote to Shanghai’s hustle.

Suzhou Silk and Couple’s Spa

Suzhou is famous for its silk, and nothing says romance like a matching pair of silk scarves or pajamas. Visit the Suzhou Silk Museum to see the entire process from silkworm to fabric. Then, book a couple’s massage at one of the boutique hotels near Jinji Lake. Many hotels offer traditional Chinese medicine-inspired treatments, such as herbal baths and acupressure, designed to balance your energy together.

Zhujiajiao: Venice of the Water Towns Without the Crowds

If Suzhou feels too polished, Zhujiajiao is the raw, authentic water town experience. Located just 45 minutes from Shanghai by car or metro, it is the perfect half-day or full-day escape.

The Fangsheng Bridge and Love Locks

Zhujiajiao is crisscrossed by nine long streets and dozens of bridges. The most famous is Fangsheng Bridge, a five-arched stone bridge built in the Ming Dynasty. Couples often pause here to take photos, but the real romantic gesture is to buy a love lock from a nearby vendor, engrave your initials, and attach it to the bridge railing. Legend says that couples who lock their love here will stay together forever. Afterward, toss the key into the water—a symbolic act of eternal commitment.

A Canal-Side Dinner for Two

Skip the crowded main street restaurants and find a small restaurant tucked along the quieter canals. Order local specialties like zongzi (sticky rice wrapped in bamboo leaves), braised pork belly, and silver fish scrambled with eggs. Many eateries have outdoor tables right on the water. As night falls, the only sounds are the lapping water and distant chatter. Share a bottle of Shaoxing rice wine, and let the moment linger.

Stay in a Heritage Guesthouse

For the full experience, book a room in a renovated Ming or Qing dynasty guesthouse. Many have private courtyards with koi ponds and moon gates. Waking up to the sound of birds and the smell of morning tea is pure bliss. Some guesthouses even offer breakfast delivered to your room in traditional bamboo baskets. It is intimate, quiet, and deeply romantic.

Hangzhou: The Lake, The Tea, and The Legend

Hangzhou is the crown jewel of romantic getaways near Shanghai. A one-hour high-speed train ride takes you to West Lake, a place that has inspired poets, emperors, and lovers for centuries.

West Lake at Dawn and Dusk

The magic of West Lake lies in its changing moods. Rent a tandem bicycle and cycle the 10-kilometer lake loop early in the morning when mist hangs over the water. Stop at the Broken Bridge, a famous spot from the legend of the White Snake, where lovers are said to meet again. At sunset, take a private boat to the center of the lake. The Three Pools Mirroring the Moon pavilion is especially beautiful when the moon is full. Many boat operators will let you bring your own wine and snacks—a floating picnic for two.

Longjing Tea Plantations: A Sensory Escape

Just a 20-minute drive from West Lake lies the Longjing (Dragon Well) tea village. The terraced hillsides are stunningly green, and the air smells of fresh leaves. Visit a local tea farmer’s home for a tasting. You will learn how to pick the tender leaves and watch them being pan-fired. The ritual of brewing tea together—warming the cups, pouring the water, and watching the leaves unfurl—is meditative. Buy a small bag of first-harvest Longjing tea as a souvenir. It is a gift that keeps giving, with each sip reminding you of the trip.

A Stay at a Lakeside Boutique Hotel

Hangzhou has no shortage of luxury hotels, but for romance, choose a boutique property with a view of West Lake. Hotels like the Amanfayun or the Four Seasons offer private gardens, infinity pools, and spa suites. Request a room with a balcony overlooking the lake. In the evening, order room service and watch the light show on the water. Some hotels also offer couple’s Tai Chi lessons at dawn—a gentle, shared activity that brings harmony to both body and relationship.

Moganshan: Mountain Air and Bamboo Forests

For couples who love nature and adventure, Moganshan is the ultimate escape. Located about two hours from Shanghai, this mountain retreat was a summer resort for Western missionaries and Shanghai elites in the early 20th century. Today, it is a haven of bamboo forests, hiking trails, and chic villas.

Hike Through the Bamboo Sea

The bamboo forests of Moganshan are legendary. Trails range from easy walks to challenging climbs. The most popular route is the one that leads to the “Sword Pond,” a small lake surrounded by towering bamboo. Legend says this is where a sword maker tested his blades. For couples, the hike is a metaphor for teamwork—help each other over steep sections, share water, and pause to listen to the wind rustling through the leaves. The sound is hypnotic.

Stay in a Converted Villa

Moganshan is famous for its “villa culture.” Many of the original stone villas have been converted into boutique hotels. The Naked Stables resort is a top choice, offering treehouses and villas with private pools. But for a more intimate experience, book a room at the Moganshan Lodge or a smaller guesthouse. Many have fireplaces, perfect for cool autumn evenings. Curl up with a book and a glass of red wine while the fire crackles.

Farm-to-Table Dining

The mountain air works up an appetite. Moganshan’s restaurants pride themselves on farm-to-table cuisine. Try bamboo shoot soup, wild mushrooms, and free-range chicken cooked in a clay pot. Some hotels offer cooking classes where you can learn to make these dishes together. The best part? The ingredients are so fresh that the flavors are unforgettable. Share a meal on a terrace overlooking the valley, and you will feel a world away from Shanghai.

Chongming Island: A Cycle Through Rural Serenity

If you want a getaway that feels truly off-the-grid, Chongming Island is the answer. Located at the mouth of the Yangtze River, this rural island is only an hour from Shanghai by car or ferry. It is flat, green, and perfect for cycling.

The Dongtan Wetland Park

Dongtan is a birdwatcher’s paradise and a photographer’s dream. Rent bicycles and ride along the wooden boardwalks that wind through the marshlands. In autumn, thousands of migratory birds stop here. The sky turns pink at sunset, and the silence is almost sacred. Bring a picnic blanket and a basket of local snacks—like Chongming rice cakes and pickled vegetables—and find a quiet spot to watch the birds.

Stay at a Farm Stay

Chongming is known for its organic farms. Many offer guesthouses where you can stay in converted barns or cottages. Wake up to the sound of roosters and the smell of fresh hay. Some farms let you pick your own vegetables or milk goats. It is a simple, grounding experience that reminds couples to appreciate the small things. In the evening, sit by a bonfire and roast sweet potatoes. No Wi-Fi, no distractions—just each other.

The Chongming Lotus Festival

If you visit in summer, you are in for a treat. The island hosts a Lotus Festival in July and August. Thousands of lotus flowers bloom in the ponds, creating a sea of pink and white. Couples can take a small boat through the lotus fields, picking flowers (with permission) and taking endless photos. The fragrance is intoxicating. It is a fleeting, seasonal beauty that mirrors the preciousness of time spent together.

Xitang: A Water Town for Night Owls

Xitang is often called the “water town for night owls” because it stays alive after dark. Located about 1.5 hours from Shanghai, it is less touristy than Zhouzhuang or Wuzhen, but equally charming.

A Boat Ride Under the Red Lanterns

Xitang’s canals are lined with red lanterns that reflect in the water like floating rubies. The best time to take a boat ride is after 8 p.m., when the day-trippers have left. The boatman will row you through narrow waterways, past ancient bridges and traditional houses. Some boats have small tables where you can play cards or sip tea. It is private, quiet, and deeply atmospheric.

Taste the Local Love Potion

Xitang is famous for its “love potion”—a sweet rice wine called “niangao jiu.” Locals say it is an aphrodisiac. Stop by a small bar along the canal and order a bottle. The wine is sweet and mild, perfect for sharing. Pair it with fried stinky tofu (trust us, it tastes better than it smells) or grilled river shrimp. The combination of flavors and the romantic setting will make the evening unforgettable.

Stay in a Canal-Side Room

Many guesthouses in Xitang have rooms with windows overlooking the canal. Book one on the second floor for the best view. Leave the window open at night so you can hear the water and the occasional distant song. Some rooms have traditional carved wooden beds and antique furniture. It feels like stepping into a different century—a perfect escape from Shanghai’s modernity.

Practical Tips for a Perfect Romantic Weekend

To make the most of your getaway, keep these tips in mind:

Book Ahead for Popular Destinations

Weekends in Hangzhou, Suzhou, and Moganshan fill up fast, especially during holidays. Book your train tickets and hotel at least two weeks in advance. For high-end resorts, a month’s notice is better.

Pack Light but Smart

Bring comfortable walking shoes, a light jacket (even in summer, evenings can be cool near water), and a small backpack for day trips. Don’t forget a camera or phone with a good camera—you will want to capture the moments.

Embrace the Local Culture

Try to learn a few phrases in Mandarin or Shanghainese. A simple “xie xie” (thank you) goes a long way. Also, respect local customs, like removing shoes before entering a guesthouse or not pointing your feet at a Buddha statue.

Disconnect to Connect

The goal of a romantic getaway is to reconnect. Put your phones on silent, or better yet, leave them in the hotel room. Focus on each other. Talk about your dreams, your memories, and your future. The beautiful surroundings are just the stage—the real magic comes from the two of you.

The Undiscovered Gems: Lesser-Known Spots for Adventurous Couples

For couples who have already visited the famous spots, consider these hidden gems:

Nanxun: The Quiet Water Town

Nanxun is less commercialized than Zhujiajiao or Xitang. It has a unique blend of Chinese and European architecture, thanks to its history as a silk trading port. Stroll through the Hundred Rooms Hall, a massive mansion with over 100 rooms, and imagine the lives of the wealthy silk merchants. The town is so quiet that you can hear the birds singing. It is perfect for couples who want solitude.

Tianmu Lake: A Lake with a View

Located near Liyang, about two hours from Shanghai, Tianmu Lake is surrounded by bamboo forests and tea plantations. The lake is shaped like a gourd, which in Chinese culture symbolizes good fortune and harmony. Rent a small boat and row to the center, or hike up to the Tianmu Lake viewing platform for a panoramic view. There are also hot springs nearby—a perfect end to a day of exploration.

Qiandao Lake: A Thousand Islands of Romance

Qiandao Lake, or Thousand Island Lake, is a man-made reservoir with over 1,000 islands. It is about three hours from Shanghai, but worth the drive. Couples can take a cruise through the islands, stopping at the most scenic ones. The water is so clear that you can see fish swimming. Some islands have small temples where you can write wishes on red ribbons and tie them to trees. It is a romantic ritual that feels almost sacred.

Final Thoughts on Romantic Getaways Near Shanghai

Shanghai’s energy is intoxicating, but love needs space to breathe. The getaways near Shanghai offer that space—whether it is the quiet canals of Zhujiajiao, the mountain air of Moganshan, or the lotus ponds of Chongming. Each destination has its own rhythm, its own flavor, and its own magic. The key is to choose one that matches your style as a couple. Do you prefer history and culture? Suzhou or Xitang. Nature and adventure? Moganshan or Chongming. Luxury and relaxation? Hangzhou or Qiandao Lake.

No matter where you go, the most important thing is that you go together. The journey, the shared meals, the laughter, and even the small mishaps (like getting lost in a bamboo forest or missing the last boat) become the stories you will tell for years. So pack your bags, grab your partner, and escape the city. Love is waiting just beyond the skyline.
