Shanghai Nightlife: Best Bars and Clubs for Australians

Home / Travel Blog / Blog Details

Shanghai is a city that never sleeps, and for Australians looking to experience a night out that rivals Sydney's Kings Cross or Melbourne's Chapel Street, this sprawling metropolis delivers in ways that will blow your mind. From rooftop bars with skyline views that make the Sydney Harbour look modest, to underground clubs pumping techno until the sun rises over the Huangpu River, Shanghai's nightlife scene is a kaleidoscope of energy, elegance, and eccentricity. Whether you're a backpacker fresh off a flight from Brisbane, a business traveler from Perth, or a digital nomad from Byron Bay, this guide will walk you through the best bars and clubs tailored to Australian tastes—where the beer is cold, the vibes are warm, and the nights are legendary.

Why Shanghai Nightlife Appeals to Australians

Australians love a good night out, and Shanghai offers a unique blend of familiarity and exoticism. You'll find craft beer bars that rival those in Melbourne's Fitzroy, cocktail lounges as sophisticated as Sydney's CBD, and dance floors that go harder than anything in Surfers Paradise. The city's international crowd means English is spoken everywhere, and the locals are incredibly welcoming. Plus, the cost of a night out in Shanghai is often a fraction of what you'd pay in Australia—think $5 pints of imported beer instead of $12, and cocktails that won't break the bank. The sheer variety is staggering: one night you can sip a negroni in a 1920s Art Deco bar, and the next you can rage at a club with laser shows and confetti cannons. For Australians craving adventure without sacrificing comfort, Shanghai is a dream destination.

Best Bars for Australians in Shanghai

The Rooster: A Taste of Home

If you're homesick for a proper Aussie pub, The Rooster is your sanctuary. Located in the former French Concession, this bar is run by an Australian expat who knows exactly what his countrymen miss: cold Victoria Bitter on tap, meat pies, and a rugby match on the big screen. The vibe is relaxed and unpretentious, with wooden tables, dim lighting, and a jukebox that plays everything from AC/DC to Tame Impala. The crowd is a mix of expats and locals who appreciate the laid-back atmosphere. Don't miss the Sunday roast—it's the closest thing to a pub lunch in Bondi. For Australians who want a familiar corner of Shanghai, The Rooster is a must-visit.

Bar Constellation: Craft Beer Heaven

Australians have a deep love for craft beer, and Bar Constellation delivers with over 50 taps rotating through local and international brews. Located in the Jing'an district, this bar feels like a collaboration between a Melbourne laneway bar and a Berlin biergarten. The staff are knowledgeable and happy to recommend a hazy IPA or a sour ale that'll remind you of your favorite brewery in Fremantle. The outdoor terrace is perfect for warm summer nights, and the indoor space has a cozy, industrial vibe with exposed brick and steel beams. They also serve excellent bar snacks, including loaded fries and burgers that rival anything from a Sydney gastropub. If you're a beer enthusiast, this is your Shangri-La.

The Long Bar at the Waldorf Astoria: Classic Elegance

For Australians who appreciate a touch of class, The Long Bar at the Waldorf Astoria on the Bund is an experience you won't forget. This bar is modeled after the original Long Bar in Shanghai's 1920s heyday, with dark wood paneling, leather armchairs, and a 34-meter-long counter that feels like a set from The Great Gatsby. The cocktail menu focuses on classic recipes, and the negroni is perfection. The crowd is sophisticated—think business travelers, wealthy locals, and couples on romantic dates. While it's pricier than most bars in Shanghai, the ambiance is worth every yuan. For Australians who want to dress up and feel like old-world royalty, this is the spot.

Speak Low: Hidden Cocktail Mastery

Australians love a speakeasy, and Speak Low is one of the best in Shanghai. Hidden behind a fake bookshelf in a nondescript building on Fuzhou Road, this bar requires a bit of detective work to find—part of the fun. Once inside, you're greeted by a cozy, dimly lit space with a menu of inventive cocktails that change seasonally. The bartenders are true artists, using techniques like fat-washing, smoking, and infusing to create drinks that are as beautiful as they are delicious. Try the "Oolong Tea Collins" or the "Smoky Old Fashioned." The vibe is intimate and mysterious, perfect for a date or a quiet night with friends. For Australians who love a hidden gem, Speak Low delivers.

The Nest: A Party Bar with a View

Perched on the 60th floor of the Shanghai Tower, The Nest offers panoramic views of the city that will make your jaw drop. This bar is all about energy—loud music, colorful cocktails, and a crowd that's ready to party. The interior is sleek and modern, with floor-to-ceiling windows that make the skyline feel like a living painting. The cocktail list is extensive, with playful names like "Shanghai Surprise" and "Bund Breeze." The food menu is equally impressive, with Asian-fusion tapas that pair perfectly with the drinks. For Australians who want to combine sightseeing with a night out, The Nest is unbeatable. Just be prepared for a line on weekends—this place is popular.

Best Clubs for Australians in Shanghai

M1NT: Luxury and Exclusivity

M1NT is the kind of club that makes you feel like a VIP. Located on the Bund, this multi-level venue features a shark tank (yes, real sharks), a rooftop terrace with stunning views, and a dance floor that's always packed with beautiful people. The dress code is strict—no sneakers, no shorts—so Australians should leave their thongs at the hotel. The music is a mix of EDM, hip-hop, and house, with international DJs spinning regularly. The crowd is glamorous and well-heeled, making it a great place to see and be seen. If you're celebrating a special occasion or just want to feel like a rockstar, M1NT is the place.

Bar Rouge: Iconic and Unforgettable

Bar Rouge is a Shanghai institution, and for good reason. This club on the Bund has been a nightlife staple for over a decade, known for its electric atmosphere, themed parties, and stunning views of the Pudong skyline. The interior is all red velvet, chandeliers, and gold accents—pure decadence. The music ranges from commercial hits to deep house, and the dance floor is always packed. The crowd is a mix of expats, tourists, and locals, all ready to party until 4 AM. For Australians who want a classic Shanghai club experience, Bar Rouge is a rite of passage.

Arkham: Underground Vibes

If you're an Australian who prefers underground techno and industrial spaces to glitzy clubs, Arkham is your spot. Located in a former factory in the French Concession, this venue is all about raw energy—concrete floors, exposed pipes, and a sound system that rattles your bones. The music is heavy on techno, house, and experimental electronic, with local and international DJs pushing the boundaries. The crowd is young, artsy, and serious about dancing. There's no VIP section, no bottle service—just pure, unadulterated music. For Australians who want to escape the tourist trap and dive into Shanghai's underground scene, Arkham is a must.

Linx: High-Tech Party Palace

Linx is a club that feels like a spaceship. Located in the Jing'an district, this venue is all about cutting-edge technology—laser shows, LED screens, and a state-of-the-art sound system that will blow your mind. The interior is futuristic, with neon lights and sleek, minimalist design. The music is a mix of EDM, pop, and hip-hop, with performances by international artists. The crowd is young and energetic, and the party often goes until dawn. For Australians who love a high-energy, visually stunning club experience, Linx delivers.

The Pearl: A Night of Cabaret and Dance

The Pearl is a unique venue that combines a theater, a cabaret, and a nightclub. Located in a historic building in the French Concession, this spot hosts everything from burlesque shows to live bands to DJ sets. The interior is vintage and elegant, with red curtains, chandeliers, and a balcony that overlooks the stage. The crowd is diverse—expats, locals, and tourists—all enjoying the eclectic entertainment. For Australians who want a night that's more than just dancing, The Pearl offers a memorable mix of performance and party.

Tips for Australians Navigating Shanghai Nightlife

Getting Around

Shanghai's metro runs until around 10:30 PM, so for late nights, you'll need to rely on taxis or ride-hailing apps like DiDi (the Chinese equivalent of Uber). Taxis are affordable and easy to hail, but make sure you have the address in Chinese written down. Many bars and clubs are in the Jing'an, French Concession, or Bund areas, so plan your night around those neighborhoods.

Dress Code

While some clubs like M1NT have strict dress codes, most bars are casual. Australians should avoid wearing thongs (flip-flops) or singlets to upscale venues. For clubs, a collared shirt and nice jeans will get you in almost anywhere. For bars, casual is fine, but it's always better to be slightly overdressed than underdressed.

Language

English is widely spoken in Shanghai's nightlife scene, especially in bars and clubs popular with expats. However, learning a few Chinese phrases like "gan bei" (cheers) and "xie xie" (thank you) can go a long way. Most menus have English translations, and bartenders are used to international customers.

Safety

Shanghai is one of the safest cities in the world for nightlife. Violent crime is rare, and you can walk home alone at 3 AM without feeling unsafe. However, watch out for drink spiking in crowded clubs—always keep an eye on your drink. Also, be aware of scams, like taxi drivers overcharging tourists. Use ride-hailing apps to avoid this.

Currency and Payment

China is largely cashless, and most places accept Alipay or WeChat Pay. As a tourist, you can link your international credit card to Alipay, but it's easier to carry some cash for small purchases. Many bars and clubs accept credit cards, but it's always good to have backup.

The Australian Expat Community in Shanghai

Shanghai has a thriving Australian expat community, and you'll find plenty of bars and clubs that cater to them. The Australian Chamber of Commerce in Shanghai hosts regular events, and there are Facebook groups where Aussies organize meetups. If you're looking for a taste of home, check out the Australian-themed bars like The Rooster or Bondi Beach Bar. The community is welcoming and always happy to help new arrivals navigate the city.

Final Thoughts on Shanghai Nightlife for Australians

Shanghai's nightlife is a world of its own, offering everything from cozy pubs to high-tech clubs. For Australians, the city provides an exciting blend of familiar comforts and new adventures. Whether you're sipping a craft beer at Bar Constellation, dancing until dawn at Arkham, or enjoying a classic cocktail at The Long Bar, you'll find that Shanghai's nightlife is as diverse and dynamic as the city itself. So grab your friends, put on your dancing shoes, and get ready for a night you won't forget. Shanghai is waiting.
